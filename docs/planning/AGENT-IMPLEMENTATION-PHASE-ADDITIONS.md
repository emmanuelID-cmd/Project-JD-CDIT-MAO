## Preserve the approved team structure

Keep Coordinator, Triage, and Escalation in Manny's lane; Knowledge, Troubleshooting, and Documentation in Shawn's lane; Shared Lane owns only interfaces, state contracts, routing connections, and end-to-end checks. The human reviewer is not an agent. The optional second reviewer remains deferred. These additions do not adopt a two-LLM-step architecture or remove the blackboard, urgency paths, or any agent. Any reduction in workload or responsibility needs a separate project-lead decision.

## Decisions and open items

The following should be resolved in the PRD-v2 planning sequence rather than silently embedded as implementation behavior:

- **Prototype action authority:** keep the current prototype propose-only and read-only. Move “agent may send/set state/execute/resolve” examples into a clearly non-MVP/post-MVP policy section while preserving the user's detailed case-scenario material as future policy context. Do not add a write tool. This resolves the conflict between those examples and the current read-only prototype tool boundary.
- **High-urgency notification (decision recorded):** show an in-app P1 status flag for tickets requiring immediate human attention; outbound email remains deferred. Define the band thresholds in the post-v2 design discussion.
- **Role model (decision recorded):** exactly three synthetic categories—User (client-facing view), Staff (back-end support view), Admin (same back-end plus privileged account/access controls). No rank ladder, separate Staff interfaces, or access-only zones. Admin may grant/update/promote/demote/suspend/deactivate/remove accounts or permissions; this does not grant agent write/resolution authority. Production identity-provider authentication remains out of scope. UC-06 currently covers denial more clearly than successful admin management.
- **Product Health (decision recorded):** retain only minimal synthetic/system metadata useful for internal investigation, corrections, and change planning; do not collect or retain actual user/ticket records. No real-world interview or field activity is required for this assignment.
- **Second reviewer and parallel arbitration:** preserve existing six-agent owners; do not add a second-reviewer agent unless a separate decision and evaluation support it.
- **Regulatory boundary:** the current proposed evaluation uses synthetic tickets/knowledge and public study metadata, not private participant or customer records. On that stated design, counsel review is not a gate to planning or the synthetic-only prototype. Do not claim that HIPAA, 21 CFR Part 11, GCP, or other rules categorically apply or categorically do not apply. Reassess with CDIT compliance/privacy counsel before accepting real user tickets/attachments or PHI, integrating into an operational CTMS/support workflow, or relying on outputs/records for regulated clinical-trial activity. The applicable scope depends on the data, parties, system purpose, and records relied on. Keep the question in the future-use gate.
- **Single-call baseline (optional, not a deliverable):** one model call processes the same synthetic case/evidence as the six-agent workflow. Comparing outputs can estimate whether MAO adds quality relative to latency/cost, but requires no extra agent and does not change architecture. Skip unless the assignment needs evidence specifically comparing MAO to a single-call approach; if run, use synthetic fixtures only and the already-approved model/data boundary.

## Regulatory reference basis

- HHS describes HIPAA PHI protections in relation to covered entities and business associates that create, receive, maintain, or transmit PHI; a synthetic/public-only prototype does not itself establish that relationship. See [HHS HIPAA and cloud computing guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/health-information-technology/cloud-computing/index.html).
- FDA explains that Part 11 applies to electronic records maintained or submitted under applicable FDA record requirements; clinical-investigation guidance identifies records needed to reconstruct a trial as examples. See [FDA Part 11 scope guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/part-11-electronic-records-electronic-signatures-scope-and-application) and [FDA clinical-investigation electronic systems Q&A](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/electronic-systems-electronic-records-and-electronic-signatures-clinical-investigations-questions).
- ICH describes GCP in connection with planning, conducting, recording, oversight, and reporting clinical trials. Whether this support-assistant prototype participates in those activities is a project/compliance determination, not assumed from the CTMS domain alone. See [ICH E6(R3) GCP material](https://admin.ich.org/sites/default/files/elearning-contents/7d11de81-debd-48cd-9c3b-dc92e7dc261c/ICH%20E6%28R3%29%20Module%201.1/story.html).
